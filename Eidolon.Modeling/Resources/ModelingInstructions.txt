primitive(name, kind, location, dimensions, rotation=(0, 0, 0))
  kind is cube, uv_sphere, ico_sphere, cylinder, cone, or torus. Dimensions are positive local lengths before rotation. Rotations are degrees around X, Y, and Z.
mesh(name, vertices, faces, location=(0, 0, 0))
  Vertices are local XYZ triples. Faces are lists of zero-based vertex indices with consistent outward winding. Use triangles, quads, or suitable planar polygons.
outline_plate(name, origin, across, up, outline, thickness, edge_radius=0, z_span=None, exposure=None)
  Makes a thin solid from an ordered, simple local 2D contour of (horizontal, vertical) pairs. The world point is origin + horizontal * across + vertical * up. Give 3 to 128 distinct points around the contour. An optional z_span of (bottom_world_z, top_world_z) fits the tilted contour to those heights without changing X or Y. The span applies to the middle plane, before centered thickness is added. edge_radius may round the edge up to half the thickness.
  Optional exposure is (target_name, outward_XYZ, [visible_outline_indices], gap) or the same tuple with a fifth value, maximum_shift. Give a horizontal direction pointing from the target toward the view where the part should show, and a small positive gap. Set a positive maximum_shift in world units when another reference limits how far the piece may move. An X shift must leave the plate inside its intended front and rear silhouette, while a Y shift must leave it inside its intended side silhouettes. The kit moves the plate within that limit and the depth of the unmarked attachment points. It preserves shape, orientation and Z span. The attachment must start inside the target. If this still hides a lobe, adjust the contour or plane so the lobe sits farther out than the attachment rather than moving the whole plate farther.
loft(name, rings, location=(0, 0, 0))
  Joins at least two ordered rings of local XYZ triples with quads and caps both ends. Every ring needs the same number of distinct points (at least three). Start each ring at the same relative point, and list points counterclockwise when looking from the last ring toward the first so the faces point out. Do not repeat the first point to close a ring. Use simple rings with planar ends; the call does not realign points, detect intersections, or add branches.
radial_loft(name, rings, rim=None, segments=48, closed=True, profile_steps=3, cover_target=None, clearance=0, section_power=2, rim_roll=0)
  Creates a smooth Z-up loft from 2 to 32 ascending rings (center_x, center_y, z, radius_x, radius_y). Radii are positive. rim optionally gives 3 to 48 increasing (angle_degrees, z) or (angle_degrees, z, radius_scale) controls, all below the top ring. 0 degrees faces -Y, 90 faces +X, 180 faces +Y, 270 faces -X. The rim is interpolated across 360 degrees and the specified angles become mesh columns. The radius scale is 0.5 to 1.75, defaults to 1, and tapers to 1 toward the top. Each angular strip runs from its rim height to the top, sampling the ring profile at the actual height. profile_steps controls sampling between rings, from 1 for a straight profile to 4 for more smooth samples. Set closed to False for an open lower edge. The upper end is capped. Use 12 to 96 base segments.
  An optional cover_target specifies an existing mesh. The side walls fit outward where the target would cross them, keeping at least clearance at the radial hit samples. This does not change the top or bottom heights or check planar caps. Set clearance in world units.
  section_power defaults to 2 for elliptical sections. Values from 2 to 4 progressively make the sections more rectangular with rounded corners; 1.5 to 2 makes them narrower toward the diagonals. This does not change the listed width or depth radii.
  A shaped rim can use rim_roll from 0 to 1 to round the projecting lobes just above the edge. It uses the individual rim radius scales above 1 for local volume and leaves the tip positions and upper contour fixed. Start around 0.25 when the reference shows a soft convex tip. Higher values can leave a noticeable band above the lower edge when successive tips have very different heights.
loft_path(name, sections, across=(1, 0, 0), segments=24, beyond=None)
  Builds a capped loft from 2 to 256 ordered sections, each (center_xyz, width_radius, height_radius) in world units. Use positive radii and 3 to 64 vertices per ring. across sets the first ring's width direction and must not be parallel to the first path segment. The ring axes follow the bends without being reset at each section. Adjacent centers must differ; avoid reversing direction at one section. Smooth shading and subdivision are separate calls. The command does not detect self-intersections.
  When a tip should visibly extend beyond an existing mesh, give beyond as (target_name, "X" or "Y", visible_length). The kit measures that target's outermost point at the tip's Z and extends the path only as needed. It keeps the first section fixed. X constrains the front/back silhouette; Y constrains the side silhouette.
blend_volumes(name, source_names, voxel_size)
  Copies the evaluated faces of 2 to 16 distinct, overlapping closed meshes into a new editable mesh and joins them with Blender voxel remesh. Use a voxel size of at least 0.0001 world units and keep overlaps thicker than the voxel size. The grid and mesh size are bounded; empty or disconnected results fail. Remeshing can soften edges and erase thin features. Sources, their visibility, and their materials stay as they were. Use visible on sources only when they should be hidden and assign_material separately on the result. UVs, materials, and instance geometry are not copied.
sdf_body(name, volumes, blend_radius, cell_size)
  Creates an editable mesh from 1 to 16 axis-aligned ellipsoids, each ((center_x, center_y, center_z), (radius_x, radius_y, radius_z)). Centers, radii, blend radius, and cell size use world units; no source objects or materials are created. The signed field uses radial surface distance outside each ellipsoid and normalized radial depth times its smallest radius inside. It combines fields in input order with a polynomial smooth minimum and extracts the zero surface with marching tetrahedra, without voxel remeshing. Use positive radii and blend radius and a cell size from 0.0001 up to the smallest ellipsoid radius. The grid is limited to 256 samples per axis, 1,500,000 total samples, 18,000,000 field evaluations, and 400,000 triangles. Overlap ellipsoids for a continuous body; distant volumes can yield separate pieces in the same mesh. Coarse sampling can lose thin joins. Apply smooth and materials separately as needed.

  Creates a new Principled material with a unique name. RGBA, metallic, and roughness use values from 0 to 1.
update_material(name, rgba=None, metallic=None, roughness=None)
  Changes only the supplied values (0 to 1) of an existing editable local material with a simple Principled shader connected directly to Material Output. Pass None to keep a value; for example, update_material('Existing', None, None, 0.7) changes only roughness. Linked or complex materials and connected inputs cannot be changed with this call.
assign_material(name, material_name, faces=None)
  Assigns a material to the whole object, or to a list of zero-based mesh face indices.
project_uv(name, projection, axes)
  Writes a dedicated surface UV map without changing geometry or existing UV maps. Call after setting the shape and transforms. PLANAR takes ordered world axes such as XZ (X is U, Z is V); use it for front-facing or broad flat areas because it projects the same map onto the far side. CYLINDRICAL takes one world axis (X, Y, or Z) for V and wraps U around it; use it on rounded parts with an axial direction. Each mesh maps to its own bounds.
apply_surface_texture(material_name, pattern, color_start, color_end, repeats=8)
  Adds one packed base-color image to an assigned local Principled material. Before this call, use project_uv on every mesh using the material and do not share it with curves or other non-mesh objects. Use a material without an existing base-color connection and set its roughness first. Colors are sRGB triples from 0 to 1. GRADIENT blends from low to high V; BANDS creates soft lines along V repeated across U; FLOW bends those lines smoothly along V; WEAVE makes crossing lines; MOTTLE makes soft variation. repeats is an integer from 1 to 48 and has no effect on GRADIENT. Use up to 16 surface textures per request. For example: apply_surface_texture('Woven surface', 'WEAVE', (0.62, 0.50, 0.34), (0.69, 0.58, 0.43), 20). The command generates the image internally; no file paths are needed.
finish_surface(name, material_name, projection, axes, pattern, strength=0.12, repeats=12)
  Projects UVs and adds subtle color variation around the current base color of a material used only by the named mesh. Use CYLINDRICAL and Z with BANDS for lengthwise tonal variation on a rounded Z-up form. PLANAR takes ordered axes such as XZ. The pattern and repeat limits match apply_surface_texture. Strength is greater than zero and at most 0.35. Call after geometry and material assignment. This counts toward the same 16-texture limit and changes no geometry.
smooth(name, enabled=True)
  Sets smooth shading for a mesh.
bevel(name, width, segments=3)
  Adds an edge bevel. Segments range from 1 to 8.
mirror(name, axis)
  Adds a Mirror modifier around the object's local origin on X, Y, or Z. It keeps the original side as well as the reflected side; duplicate followed by mirror does not create an opposite-side-only part. For a separate left/right pair, build each part at its final world position. An off-center object's origin is not the subject's centerline.
subdivide(name, levels=1)
  Adds Catmull-Clark subdivision, from 1 to 3 levels.
fit_surface(name, target_name, offset=0)
  Fits each vertex of an editable detail mesh to the nearest point on a distinct target mesh's evaluated surface. A positive offset generally moves back toward the vertex's pre-fit position; this does not determine which side is outside. Start close to the intended visible outside with no touching or crossing. Use a small positive offset for a visible patch to avoid depth fighting. Subdivide before fitting if needed, and add thin solidify afterward only if thickness is needed. To correct an existing Surface Fit, call fit_surface again on the same detail and target to update it in place.
surface_oval(name, target_name, center, horizontal, vertical, max_distance, offset, surface_error, lower_material, upper_material, minimum_scale=1, max_center_shift=0, optional=False)
  Makes a smooth, two-color oval marking that follows an existing mesh. Give the world XYZ center out in front and two XYZ half-axis vectors. Their cross product must point out of the target; for a front-facing oval use positive X and positive Z. Create materials first, and repeat the same name for a single color. Keep the full oval inside the target silhouette. The kit projects it and checks the interior. For nonessential decoration, minimum_scale and max_center_shift work as in project_surface. Passing True as the last positional argument removes just that oval on a geometric miss and logs why. Do not refer to an optional oval later. For eyes or another key landmark, leave these three options at their defaults.
optional_surface_oval(name, target_name, center, horizontal, vertical, max_distance, offset, surface_error, material_name, minimum_scale=0.75, max_center_shift=0)
  Creates an incidental single-color oval using the same axes and checks as surface_oval. If the target surface is missed, logs and omits the oval without aborting the whole scene. Put it inside the target's silhouette. Do not refer to it in later calls.
surface_stroke(name, target_name, points, ray_direction, width, material_name, max_distance, offset, surface_error)
  Makes a smooth painted line, such as a smile or a curved graphic, that is attached to a mesh. Use 2 to 32 world points in order along its centerline outside the target, with room for the full width. Set the nonzero ray direction toward the target and use a small positive offset with a smaller positive surface_error. The kit samples, projects and checks the strip. Use this instead of a beveled curve when the reference shows paint with no volume.
surface_sheet(name, targets, rows, ray_direction, max_distance, offsets, steps=4, minimum_width_scale=1, edge_clearance=0)
  Creates a smooth open mesh following the first surface along a world XYZ ray. targets contains 1–4 existing mesh names. rows contains 2–16 rows of 2–32 world XYZ control points, with equal column counts and matching order. Space the rows from root to visible edge. Keep every interpolated point outside the targets and within ray reach. If a row's edges miss, minimum_width_scale (0.5 to 1) allows narrowing around the row's center; a remaining miss fails without creating the sheet. offsets gives one signed world distance per row, positive toward the viewer and negative into the target. steps is an integer from 1 to 8. Use small edge_clearance if the curved target may clip the sheet's visible side edges. It starts at zero at the root and increases toward the outer rows. Add restrained thickness afterward if needed.
project_surface(name, target_name, ray_direction, max_distance, offset=0, surface_error=None, minimum_scale=1, max_center_shift=0)
  Projects a detail mesh along a nonzero world XYZ direction to the first visible surface of a distinct evaluated mesh. Start the entire patch outside within positive max_distance in world units. A nonnegative offset moves each hit back against the ray. With surface_error omitted, topology stays unchanged. With a positive error smaller than offset, the kit samples face interiors and can split and reproject the patch until sampled gaps stay within that error of the offset. Splitting keeps material indices and shading but requires no UVs, custom attributes, or vertex groups; set face-indexed colors before projection. The limit is four splits and 16384 resulting triangles when splitting. When a vertex misses and minor placement changes are acceptable, pass minimum_scale (0.5 to 1) for uniform shrinking and max_center_shift for the allowed distance toward the center of the target bounds, in the plane perpendicular to the ray. Both default to preserving placement. A missed ray after fitting or exhausted refinement fails without changing the source. No source modifiers or shape keys.
check_surface_gap(name, target_name, ray_direction, max_gap, max_inset=0)
  Checks evaluated vertices and samples along triangle edges and interiors against the first hit on a distinct target mesh. max_gap is the positive allowed distance in front; max_inset is the nonnegative allowed distance behind, in world units along the ray. A missed ray or out-of-range sample fails. This does not edit either mesh or certify the space between samples. Aim from outside toward the intended surface.
optional_surface_patch(name, vertices, faces, material_name, target_name, ray_direction, max_distance, offset, surface_error, minimum_scale=1, max_center_shift=0)
  Creates a new single-color patch from world-space vertices and faces, applies the existing material, and runs project_surface plus check_surface_gap. If fitting or the gap check fails geometrically, removes only this new patch and logs why. Other errors still fail the job. Do not refer to this optional object in later calls since it may not exist.
surface_diamond(name, target_name, side, height, width, length, material_name, offset, surface_error, across_position=0)
  Adds an optional diamond marking to a curved mesh. For a diamond shown on the left profile use LEFT, and RIGHT for the right profile; FRONT and BACK are also supported. Give center height in world Z and horizontal width and vertical length in world units. The kit starts the ray beyond the actual target bounds and centers the marking horizontally on that surface. across_position optionally shifts it across that view. The patch follows the surface and is omitted on a geometric miss. Use this instead of estimating XYZ ray origins for diamond markings.
attach_to_surface(name, target_name, anchor, ray_origin, ray_direction, max_distance, inset=0)
  Moves an editable object so its local XYZ anchor meets the first ray hit on a distinct mesh's evaluated surface. Ray origin and direction are world XYZ, the direction must be nonzero, and max_distance is positive in world units. An optional nonnegative inset moves the anchor farther along the ray by that many world units. A missed ray is an error. The call changes location only, supports ordinary object parents, and does not create a persistent link. It does not support active source transform constraints or a target parented under the source.
solidify(name, thickness)
  Gives surface geometry a positive thickness.
deform(name, method, axis, amount)
  Adds BEND or TWIST (amount in degrees) or TAPER or STRETCH (factor) about local X, Y, or Z.
boolean(name, cutter_name, operation)
  Adds DIFFERENCE, UNION, or INTERSECT between distinct meshes. Keep a cutter used by the modifier; hide it with visible(cutter_name, False) if it should not appear.
